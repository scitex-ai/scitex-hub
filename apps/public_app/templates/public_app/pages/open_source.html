Why Open Source Matters

The Problem with Closed Tools

Research software often disappears. Companies shut down, products get discontinued, pricing changes. When that happens, years of workflows, data formats, and institutional knowledge become obsolete.

Closed tools also create invisible dependencies. You can't verify what's happening to your data. You can't adapt the tool to your specific needs. You're trusting a black box with your research.

Our Commitment

SciTeX is 100% open source. Every line of code—from the Python toolkit to this cloud platform—is publicly available, auditable, and modifiable. We believe research infrastructure should be owned by the community, not locked behind corporate walls.

Licensed under AGPL-3.0

Why AGPL-3.0

Ensures perpetual freedom. The GNU Affero General Public License guarantees that SciTeX—and any derivative works—will remain open source forever. No one can take this code and make it proprietary.

Protects the network effect. Even if someone runs a modified version as a service, they must share their improvements. The community benefits from every enhancement.

Your outputs are yours. The license applies to the software, not to the papers, figures, or data you create with it. Your research remains your intellectual property.

What This Means for Researchers

Reproducibility without dependency. Your workflows can be reproduced by anyone, anywhere, forever—not contingent on a company's survival or pricing decisions.

Transparency in computation. See exactly how your data is processed. Audit the statistics. Verify the transformations. No hidden algorithms.

Freedom to adapt. Need a specific feature for your lab? Fork the code. Modify it. Contribute back if you want. You're not waiting on a product roadmap.

No vendor lock-in. Export your data anytime. Self-host if needed. Switch tools without losing years of work.

Long-term Vision for Science

Science advances through shared knowledge. The tools we use to produce science should follow the same principle. Closed research infrastructure creates artificial barriers—between labs, between institutions, between generations of researchers.

We envision a future where research tools are public goods: maintained by the community, improved by practitioners, trusted because they're transparent. Where a graduate student in any country has access to the same infrastructure as well-funded institutions.

Open source isn't just a license choice. It's a commitment to making scientific computing more equitable, more reproducible, and more durable.
